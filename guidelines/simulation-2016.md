# Simulation-based research extensions

Extensions to **CONSORT** (trials) and **STROBE** (observational) for health care
simulation-based research. Both halves are here: 11 of the 25 CONSORT items carry an SBR
extension (Table 1 of the source), and 10 of the 22 STROBE items do (Table 2). Items not
listed are unchanged — see `consort-2025.md` and `strobe-2007.md`.

**Version caveat.** Published against **CONSORT 2010**; the item numbers below refer to that
version. CONSORT 2025 has since restructured the checklist, so map by topic.

The item that most often decides whether SBR is interpretable is **12** — the unit of
analysis. Simulation studies routinely randomise teams but analyse individuals, or take
repeated measures per participant without accounting for them.

Cite as `[@cheng2016simulation]`.

# CONSORT extensions (trials)

## Title and abstract — CONSORT item 1

### Item 1 — Extension

In abstract or key terms, the MESH or searchable keyword term must have the word "simulation" or "simulated".

## Introduction — CONSORT item 2

### Item 2 — Extension

Clarify whether simulation is subject of research or investigational method for research.

## Methods: interventions — CONSORT item 5

### Item 5 — Extension

Describe the theoretical and/or conceptual rationale for the design of each intervention. Clearly describe all simulation-specific exposures, potential confounders, and effect modifiers.

## Methods: outcomes — CONSORT item 6

### Item 6 — Extension

In describing the details of methods of assessment, include (when applicable) the setting, instrument, simulator type, timing in relation to the intervention, along with any methods used to enhance the quality of measurements. Provide evidence to support the validity and reliability of assessment tools in this context (if available).

## Methods: blinding — CONSORT item 11

### Item 11 — Extension

Describe strategies to decrease risk of bias, when blinding is not possible.

## Methods: statistical methods — CONSORT item 12

### Item 12 — Extension

Clearly indicate the unit of analysis (eg, individual, team, system), identify repeated measures on subjects, and describe how these issues were addressed.

## Results: baseline data — CONSORT item 15

### Item 15 — Extension

In describing characteristics of study participants, include their previous experience with simulation and other relevant features as related to the intervention(s).

## Results: outcomes and estimation — CONSORT item 17

### Item 17 — Extension

For assessments involving more than one rater, interrater reliability should be reported.

## Discussion: limitations — CONSORT item 20

### Item 20 — Extension

Specifically discuss the limitations of SBR.

## Discussion: generalizability — CONSORT item 21

### Item 21 — Extension

Describe generalizability of simulation-based outcomes to patient-based outcomes (if applicable).

## Other information: funding — CONSORT item 25

### Item 25 — Extension

List simulator brand and if conflict of interest for intellectual property exists.

# STROBE extensions (observational studies)

## Title and abstract — STROBE item 1

### Item 1 — Extension

In abstract or key terms, the MESH or searchable keyword term must have the word simulation or simulated.

## Introduction: background/rationale — STROBE item 2

### Item 2 — Extension

Clarify whether simulation is subject of research or investigational method for research.

## Methods: variables — STROBE item 7

### Item 7 — Extension

Describe the theoretical and/or conceptual rationale for the design of the intervention/exposure. Describe the intervention/exposure with sufficient detail to permit replication. Clearly describe all simulation-specific exposures, potential confounders, and effect modifiers.

## Methods: data sources/measurement — STROBE item 8

### Item 8 — Extension

In describing the details of methods of assessment, include (when applicable) the setting, instrument, simulator type, timing in relation to the intervention, along with any methods used to enhance the quality of measurements. Provide evidence to support the validity and reliability of assessment tools in this context (if available).

## Methods: statistical methods — STROBE item 12

### Item 12 — Extension

Clearly indicate the unit of analysis (e.g., individual, team, system), identify repeated measures on subjects, and describe how these issues were addressed.

## Results: descriptive data — STROBE item 14

### Item 14 — Extension

In describing characteristics of study participants, include their previous experience with simulation and other relevant features as related to the intervention(s).

## Results: main results — STROBE item 16

### Item 16 — Extension

For assessments involving more than one rater, interrater reliability should be reported.

## Discussion: limitations — STROBE item 19

### Item 19 — Extension

Specifically discuss the limitations of SBR.

## Discussion: generalizability — STROBE item 21

### Item 21 — Extension

Describe generalizability of simulation-based outcomes to patient-based outcomes (if applicable).

## Other information: funding — STROBE item 22

### Item 22 — Extension

Give the source of funding and the role of the funders for the present study and, if applicable, for the original study on which the present article is based. List simulator brand and if conflict of interest for intellectual property exists.
