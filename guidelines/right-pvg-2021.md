# RIGHT for PVG

17 items across 12 topics, for the **patient- and public-facing version** of a clinical
practice guideline — the plain-language booklet derived from a full guideline, not the
guideline itself. For the guideline proper use `right-2017.md`.

The distinguishing requirements, which have no counterpart in RIGHT: item 6.1 (link back to
the source guideline so the evidence and recommendation-development methods remain
traceable), 8.1 (explain what strength-of-recommendation and certainty-of-evidence terms
actually mean, in language a reader without training can follow), 9.1 (questions for
patients to ask their clinicians), and 10.1 (a glossary).

**Note on the source.** The published Table 1 pairs each item with a lengthy worked example
drawn from real patient booklets (NCCN, SIGN, NICE, ESMO). Those examples are illustrative,
not normative, and run to several pages. Only the item text is reproduced here; consult the
paper for the examples.

Cite as `[@wang2021rightpvg]`.

## Basic information

### Item 1.1 — Title/cover/copyright

Identify the document as a guideline version for patients and the public.

### Item 1.2 — Title/cover/copyright

Specify the topic (e.g., condition, technique, or medication) addressed in the PVG.

### Item 1.3 — Title/cover/copyright

Specify the publication year and the version (if applicable) (e.g., first version, second version) of the PVG in the title, cover page, or copyright statement.

### Item 2.1 — Contact information

Provide contact information of the developers of the PVG (e.g., affiliations, website, or address, phone number, or email address).

### Item 3.1 — Summary

Provide a summary of the PVG, including the main recommendations.

## Background

### Item 4.1 — Introduction of the target topic

Introduce the target condition, including (as relevant) the definition, risk factors, signs, subtypes, complications, staging (progress), and epidemiology.

### Item 4.2 — Introduction of the target topic

Introduce the management, preventive, diagnostic, and other options.

### Item 5.1 — Purpose, scope, and target users

Describe the scope, purpose, intended use, and users of the PVG.

### Item 6.1 — Link to the source guideline

Provide a reference or link to the source guideline of the PVG, where the methods of the source guideline (e.g., the evidence review and recommendation development process) can be found.

## Recommendations

### Item 7.1 — Recommendations

Include for each recommendation:

- (a) the target populations or conditions;
- (b) the recommended treatment or management option (e.g., prevention plan, diagnostic strategy, or rehabilitation);
- (c) potential benefits and harms, especially those that are patient important; and
- (d) the specific settings where the options are recommended to be implemented.

### Item 7.2 — Recommendations

Describe what options, if any, are available to deal with undesirable outcomes.

### Item 7.3 — Recommendations

Describe the self-management options, if any are reported in the source guideline.

### Item 8.1 — The strength of the recommendations and certainty of the evidence

Provide a clear and simple explanation of the meaning of terms related to the strength of recommendations and quality of the evidence (e.g., by using commonly understood symbols).

## Other information

### Item 9.1 — Questions to ask

Suggest a list of questions for patients to ask their healthcare providers if relevant.

### Item 10.1 — Terms and abbreviations

Provide a list of terms and abbreviations used in the PVG.

### Item 11.1 — Funding

Describe the funding source(s) of the PVG and of the source guideline and their roles or any influences, in the PVG or guideline development processes, respectively.

### Item 12.1 — Conflicts of interest

Report the conflicts of interests of contributors to the PVG and the source guideline in a format that the patients and the public can understand, and how they were managed.
